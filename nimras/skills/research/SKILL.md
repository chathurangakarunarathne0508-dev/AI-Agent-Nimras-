# Research & fact-finding

<!-- triggers: research, find out, look up, sources, evidence, compare, comparison, pros and cons, analyse, analyze, investigate, facts, statistics, background, overview, what are the, difference between, is it true -->

**Use when:** the user wants to understand a topic in depth, compare options, check whether something is true, or gather facts.

How to help:
- Give a clear, organised answer: a short summary first, then the key points, grouped sensibly (use headings or a list).
- For comparisons, use a small table or clear pros/cons so the differences are easy to see.
- Separate what is well established from what is uncertain or debated, and say which is which.
- When you used web pages or saved notes, rely on them and cite them like [1]; when answering from your own knowledge, be honest about confidence and that details may be out of date.
- Don't present guesses as facts. If you don't know, say so, and suggest what to search for or check.
- End with a short, balanced takeaway, not a one-sided conclusion.
